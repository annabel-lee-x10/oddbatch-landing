# oddbatch.app: Scope and Execution Plan

**Status:** Plan only. Nothing built, no repo created.
**Author:** Opus 5 planning pass
**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Executor:** Sonnet (see section 8 for the sequenced handoff)

---

## 0. What this is

A one-page landing site at `oddbatch.app`. A neutral shelf for small independent apps. It is not a Root-branded property and it does not claim ownership of anything it lists. It sits beside the `quietbuild.ai` family (Munki, Blessroot, Practica, Snipcraft, CV Builder, Quiet Stash) but shares no branding with it, on purpose. Quietbuild is a maker's portfolio. Oddbatch is a shelf anyone can end up on.

The single hard constraint that shapes every decision below: **oddbatch must never read as a company that made the apps.** Curation, not authorship. Every copy and design choice is checked against that.

## 1. Brand direction

### Voice, one line

Plain, unhurried, slightly dry. It talks like a shopkeeper who is not trying to sell you anything, only pointing at a shelf.

Rules for anyone writing copy here:
- No growth language. No "discover", "curated experiences", "the home for". No exclamation marks.
- Short declaratives. Fragments are fine.
- Never speak on behalf of the apps. "Built by someone else" is a feature, say it plainly.
- One joke maximum on the whole page, and it should be dry enough to miss.

### Palette

Keep the warm direction from the draft. It is correct: warm off-white reads as paper and shelf, not as SaaS. Slight refinements for contrast and for a dark mode later.

| Token | Hex | Use | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#f7f5f0` | page background | Unchanged from draft. Warm paper. Reads analog, non-corporate. |
| `--surface` | `#efece4` | app card / row background | One step down from bg. Gives shelf rows a body without borders everywhere. |
| `--ink` | `#191817` | primary text | Slightly warmed black. Pure `#000` on warm paper looks like a rendering bug. |
| `--muted` | `#6b6862` | secondary text, labels | Draft value nudged warmer to sit in the same family as bg. Passes AA on `--bg` at roughly 4.9:1. |
| `--line` | `#ddd8cb` | hairlines, dividers | Draft value. Visible but never structural. |
| `--accent` | `#b4472e` | one mark, links on hover | Draft value, keep it. Muted brick red. Not a brand red, not a CTA red. Reads as ink stamp. |
| `--accent-soft` | `#e8d5cd` | rare fill, focus rings | Tint of accent for focus states that do not shout. |

Accent discipline: the accent appears at most twice on the page. The wordmark dot and link hover. If a third accent shows up, delete one.

Dark mode: not for v0. If added later, invert to `#16150f` bg / `#f0ede4` ink / accent lifted to `#d4674a` for contrast. Do not ship dark mode until there is actual content on the shelf; a dark empty state looks broken.

### Typography

Keep Fraunces plus JetBrains Mono. The pairing is doing real work: the serif carries warmth and gives an indie shelf a human register, and the mono handles labels and metadata so the page reads as made by someone technical without being a terminal cosplay.

- **Fraunces** (variable, weights 400 and 600, optical size axis). Headings and body. Set `opsz` high on the h1 so the display cut shows character, and lower on body for readability. Italic on two words of the hero only.
- **JetBrains Mono** (400, 500). Wordmark, section labels, footer, empty state, app metadata. Never body copy.
- Load exactly these two families and only the weights used. Two font files, self-hosted, subset to latin. Do not add a third face.

Fallbacks: `Fraunces, 'Iowan Old Style', Georgia, serif` and `'JetBrains Mono', ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace`.

### Wordmark

Lowercase `oddbatch`, JetBrains Mono, letter-spaced, with the accent dot preceding it. Keep the draft treatment. It is a good mark: the dot reads as a status light, a bullet, a full stop, and a pin on a shelf, all of which are on-theme, and it costs nothing to render.

Rules:
- Always lowercase. Never `OddBatch`, never `Oddbatch` in running text.
- The dot is 6px, accent, always to the left, never inside the word.
- No logotype file needed for v0. The mark is CSS. If a favicon is needed, it is the dot alone on `--bg`.

### Keep or pivot

**Keep the draft's direction.** The warm serif choice is the single thing making this not look like every other indie directory. The pivot risk was toward a mono-only brutalist look, which is the default aesthetic for this category and would make oddbatch invisible next to it. Refine, do not replace.

Refinements to the draft:
1. Add the `--surface` token so shelf rows have a body.
2. Widen `main` from 620px to 660px so app rows have room for name plus one-line description plus platform tag.
3. Add a visible focus style. The draft has none.
4. Add `prefers-reduced-motion` handling to any transition (currently one, on link underline; keep it that way).
5. The `//` prefix on the empty state is a nice touch but reads as code comment, slightly off-brand for a shelf. Alternative below in section 2.

## 2. Site content, v0 launch

### Hero, three alternatives

**A. Draft, refined (recommended)**
> A quiet shelf
> for *small apps*.
>
> Independent tools built by people without marketing teams. Collected here because the good ones are hard to find.
>
> Not affiliated with any of them. Just a shelf.

Why: "shelf" is the whole brand in one noun, it carries the neutrality claim for free, and the fineprint line does the disclaimer work without a legal register.

**B. Plainer, more directory**
> Small apps,
> *found and listed*.
>
> Independent software that never got a launch post. One page, no ranking, no scores.
>
> We did not build any of these.

Why: sharper on neutrality, weaker on atmosphere. Use if Root wants the disclaimer to land harder.

**C. Drier**
> Odd little apps
> that *deserve a shelf*.
>
> Software from people who shipped it themselves. Listed here, nothing more.
>
> No affiliation, no cut, no rankings.

Why: most personality, slightly more risk of reading as twee. "no cut" preempts the "is this an affiliate farm" question, which is worth something.

**Recommendation: A**, with C's third line grafted on, so the fineprint becomes: `Not affiliated with any of them. No rankings, no cut. Just a shelf.`

### Body copy, final for v0

Sections in order:

1. **Wordmark**, top left.
2. **Hero** per A above.
3. **On the shelf** section. Either the empty state or the first hardcoded apps (see below).
4. **Submit** section:
   > Built something small and useful? Send a link and a sentence about it. `hello@oddbatch.app`
5. **Footer**: `oddbatch.app` left, `2026` right. Nothing else. No nav, no social, no newsletter.

Deliberately absent from v0: about page, manifesto, newsletter capture, social links, "how we choose" page. Every one of those is a v2 conversation and adding them now dilutes the one-page discipline.

### Empty state versus list-of-apps

Two viable v0 shapes. Pick one before Sonnet starts.

**Option 1: empty state.** Ships in a day, honest, and the anticipation reads fine on a page this small. Copy alternatives:
- `first batch, soon` (mono, muted, no `//` prefix)
- `nothing on it yet`
- `shelf is being built`

Risk: an empty shelf on an unlaunched domain looks abandoned to a first-time visitor with no context, and it gives zero reason to return.

**Option 2: two or three hardcoded apps (recommended).** A shelf with three things on it is a real site. An empty shelf is a placeholder. Three is enough to establish the format and the neutrality claim, since the reader can see they are from different makers.

Data shape per app, hardcoded as a plain array in v0:

```
{ name, oneLine, url, platform, addedOn }
```

Row render: name in Fraunces 600, one line in Fraunces 400 muted, platform in mono uppercase 11px, whole row is the link, hairline between rows.

Constraint that matters: if Root's own apps are the first three, oddbatch immediately reads as a Root property and the neutrality positioning is dead on arrival. **At most one Root app in the first batch, and it must not be first in the list.** Fill the other two with genuinely third-party indie apps Root actually likes. If that is not possible before launch, ship the empty state instead. This is the single most important content decision on the page.

### Submission CTA

`hello@oddbatch.app` as a mailto link, no form, for v0. A mailto is the lowest-maintenance intake, it filters out low-effort submissions by requiring a real email, and it costs nothing to run. Copy: `Built something small and useful? Send a link and a sentence about it.`

### SEO and meta

```
<title>oddbatch, a quiet shelf for small apps</title>
<meta name="description" content="Independent apps, collected in one place. Not affiliated with any of them.">
canonical https://oddbatch.app
og:title, og:description, og:url, og:image, og:type=website
twitter:card=summary_large_image
```

**og image plan:** one static 1200x630 PNG, checked into the repo at `/public/og.png`. Design: `--bg` background, the accent dot and `oddbatch` wordmark in mono, centered-left, and `a quiet shelf for small apps` in Fraunces beneath. No screenshot, no gradient, no device mockup. Generate it once by hand or by rendering an HTML template to PNG; do not add a dynamic OG image service for a one-page site.

Also ship: `favicon.svg` (the accent dot), `robots.txt` allowing everything, and a two-line `sitemap.xml`. Skip structured data for v0; there is no list to mark up yet. Add `ItemList` schema at v2 when the directory is real.

## 3. Tech stack

### The options for this specific case

**Static HTML, hand-written, no build**
- Pros: zero dependencies, zero build step, zero supply chain, loads instantly, will still work untouched in five years, and matches the indie aesthetic honestly rather than as a costume. The draft is already 90 percent of the site.
- Cons: adding a second page or a repeated component means copy-paste. No JSX, so Root's muscle memory does not apply. Fonts and meta tags are hand-managed.

**Next.js (App Router)**
- Pros: Root uses it everywhere, so zero context switch. Vercel deploy is a non-event. Path to v1 and v2 is already paved: a submission route, Supabase reads, dynamic app pages, all with no migration. Image and font optimization are built in.
- Cons: a `node_modules` tree and a framework upgrade treadmill for one page of static HTML. First build introduces dependencies that must be maintained for years to serve a document that never changes.

**Astro**
- Pros: the technically ideal fit. Ships zero JS by default, content-first, components when needed, grows to a directory cleanly, excellent Markdown story if apps ever become content files.
- Cons: a second framework in Root's world. Every future session on this repo pays a small context tax for framework recall. That tax is real and recurring, and it buys an advantage over Next.js that is measured in kilobytes on a page that has none to spare anyway.

### Recommendation

**Next.js (App Router), static-exported, with the page written as close to plain HTML as the framework allows.**

Reasoning, in order of weight:

1. **The stated growth path is not static.** Section 6 has a submission form at v1 and a searchable directory at v2. Both want a server. Starting on Next.js means v1 is a new route file, not a migration. Starting on static HTML means v1 is a rewrite, and rewrites at the exact moment the project gets interesting are where projects die.
2. **Consistency beats optimality here.** Root has a Next.js plus Vercel plus Supabase pattern that already works. Introducing Astro to save 8KB on a page nobody is loading over 3G is optimizing the wrong variable. The cost of a third stack is paid on every future session.
3. **Astro is genuinely better on the merits for a one-pager** and if this were the whole scope forever, it would be the pick. It is not the whole scope forever.
4. Static HTML is the right answer only if Root commits that oddbatch will never be more than one page. If that is actually true, say so and take it: it is the cheapest correct answer. Section 9 asks this directly.

Discipline conditions on the Next.js pick, non-negotiable for v0:
- No UI library. No Tailwind. One CSS file with custom properties, exactly like the draft. Tailwind on a one-page site is 100 lines of config to avoid writing 200 lines of CSS.
- No client components. The whole page is a server component that renders static HTML.
- No `next/image` for v0; there are no images except the OG file, which is never rendered in-page.
- Fonts self-hosted via `next/font/local` with `display: swap`. No Google Fonts network request. This removes the two `preconnect` tags and a third-party dependency, and it is faster.
- Dependencies at v0: `next`, `react`, `react-dom`. That is the entire list.

## 5. Deploy path

### Vercel project

1. Create the repo on GitHub, push `main`.
2. Import into Vercel as a new project named `oddbatch`. Framework preset auto-detects Next.js.
3. Build command and output stay at defaults. No environment variables at v0.
4. Production branch: `main`. Every other branch gets a preview URL automatically.
5. Turn off Vercel's deployment comment bot if it is noisy on a solo repo.

### DNS for oddbatch.app

Add the apex and `www` in Vercel's domain settings first so it prints the exact target values, then set records at the registrar (or in Cloudflare if the domain is on Cloudflare, which is the recommended path, see email below).

| Type | Name | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | `@` | `76.76.21.21` | Vercel apex. Confirm the current value in the Vercel dashboard rather than trusting this doc; Vercel has changed it before. |
| CNAME | `www` | `cname.vercel-dns.com` | Then set `www` to redirect to apex in Vercel. |

If the domain sits behind Cloudflare, set both records to **DNS only** (grey cloud), not proxied. Proxying in front of Vercel adds a second CDN, complicates certificate issuance, and buys nothing.

Canonical host: apex `oddbatch.app`, with `www` redirecting to it. Set this once and make sure the canonical meta tag agrees.

TLS is automatic once DNS resolves. Expect it inside 10 minutes; if the cert is still pending after an hour, the usual cause is a proxied record or a stale CAA record.

### Preview versus production

- `main` is production, auto-deploys, and is the only branch that touches the real domain.
- Feature branches get a `oddbatch-git-<branch>` preview URL. Use these for copy review; they are the right way to show Root a wording change without merging.
- Leave preview deployments publicly accessible. There is nothing private here and auth walls make sharing a copy tweak annoying.
- Verification rule per Root's standards: after any merge to `main`, wait for the Vercel deploy to report ready, then fetch `https://oddbatch.app` and confirm a 200 with the expected hero string in the body before reporting done. Deploy dashboard green is necessary, not sufficient.

### Email for hello@oddbatch.app

**Goal, confirmed by Root:** `hello@oddbatch.app` routes to `oddbatchapp@gmail.com`, which already exists. Inbound only. No mailbox to check separately, no new password, no new inbox habit. This is not urgent and can be done any time after the domain resolves; nothing in steps 1 through 6 depends on it.

**Recommendation: Cloudflare Email Routing.**

Why it wins for this exact case:
- Free, permanently, with no volume tier or trial clock.
- Purpose-built for the thing Root wants: address in, Gmail out. Nothing else.
- It writes its own MX and SPF records into the zone, so there is no hand-transcription step and no chance of a typo in a priority number.
- No third-party service beyond Cloudflare holds the mail path.
- Destination verification is a single click in an email Cloudflare sends to `oddbatchapp@gmail.com`, so setup ends with proof it works.

**The one hard prerequisite:** Cloudflare Email Routing only works if `oddbatch.app` uses Cloudflare nameservers. If the domain is currently on the registrar's default DNS, the nameservers must move to Cloudflare first, and the Vercel A and CNAME records from the table above move with them. That is a 10-minute job plus propagation, and it is worth doing regardless because Cloudflare's DNS editor is better than any registrar's. But it means **DNS provider and email provider are one decision, not two.** If Root does not want to move nameservers, skip to ImprovMX below, which works on any DNS.

Where the domain is registered is currently unknown. It matters only for who holds the nameserver setting. See question 9 in section 9.

#### Setup order (do it in this order or the verification step fails)

1. Add `oddbatch.app` to Cloudflare as a zone. Cloudflare imports the existing records; check that the Vercel A record for `@` and the CNAME for `www` came across, and that both are set to **DNS only** (grey cloud).
2. Change the nameservers at the registrar to the two Cloudflare assigns. Wait for Cloudflare to report the zone active.
3. Re-verify the site still loads over HTTPS at `oddbatch.app` before touching email. If the site broke, fix that first; do not stack an email change on top of a broken zone.
4. Email > Email Routing > Get started. Cloudflare offers to add the MX and TXT records automatically. Accept that. Do not add them by hand.
5. Add the destination address `oddbatchapp@gmail.com`. Cloudflare emails it a verification link. Click it from that inbox.
6. Add a custom address rule: `hello@oddbatch.app` forwards to `oddbatchapp@gmail.com`.
7. Set the catch-all action to **Drop**, not forward. Scraped addresses like `sales@` and `admin@` will start arriving within weeks of the domain being indexed; dropping them keeps the Gmail clean.

#### DNS records this creates

Cloudflare adds these itself in step 4. They are listed here so the plan is complete and so anyone auditing the zone later knows what belongs there and why.

| Type | Name | Value | Priority | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MX | `@` | `route1.mx.cloudflare.net` | see note | Inbound mail |
| MX | `@` | `route2.mx.cloudflare.net` | see note | Inbound mail |
| MX | `@` | `route3.mx.cloudflare.net` | see note | Inbound mail |
| TXT | `@` | `v=spf1 include:_spf.mx.cloudflare.net ~all` | n/a | SPF, authorises Cloudflare to handle mail for the domain |

Note on priorities: Cloudflare assigns randomised MX priorities per zone (commonly in the 10 to 90 range, different for every domain). **Do not copy priority numbers from this document.** Let Cloudflare write the records, then confirm all three MX plus the SPF TXT are present in the zone.

SPF caution: a domain must have exactly one SPF TXT record. If a second `v=spf1` record already exists on the apex for any reason, merge the includes into one record rather than adding a second. Two SPF records is a permanent-fail condition, not a warning.

DKIM and DMARC: not required for inbound forwarding and not worth adding at v0, since the domain sends no outbound mail. If outbound sending is ever added (see below), add DMARC at `_dmarc.oddbatch.app` with `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:oddbatchapp@gmail.com` first and tighten it later.

#### The one real limitation, stated plainly

Cloudflare Email Routing is **inbound only**. Submissions land in `oddbatchapp@gmail.com` fine, but replying sends **from** `oddbatchapp@gmail.com`, not from `hello@oddbatch.app`. Gmail's "Send mail as" needs an authenticated SMTP server for the domain, and Cloudflare does not provide one.

Whether that matters: probably not much. `oddbatchapp@gmail.com` is an obviously-oddbatch address, so a reply from it is not confusing, only slightly less polished. For v0, accept it. If oddbatch starts getting real submission volume and the Gmail-address reply starts feeling wrong, that is the signal to move to a real mailbox, and moving later costs one MX swap.

#### Alternatives and their tradeoffs

**ImprovMX (free tier).** The right pick if Root does not want to move nameservers. Works on any DNS provider, including the registrar's. Setup is add two MX and one TXT, then add the alias in ImprovMX's dashboard. Records:

| Type | Name | Value | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| MX | `@` | `mx1.improvmx.com` | 10 |
| MX | `@` | `mx2.improvmx.com` | 20 |
| TXT | `@` | `v=spf1 include:spf.improvmx.com ~all` | n/a |

Tradeoff: one more third-party account in the mail path, and the free tier is a free tier, meaning it can change. It also shares Cloudflare's inbound-only limitation on the free plan. Fine for this use, marginally less durable.

**Zoho Mail (free plan).** Gives a real mailbox at `hello@oddbatch.app` rather than a forward, and it is free for one domain. Records:

| Type | Name | Value | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| MX | `@` | `mx.zoho.com` | 10 |
| MX | `@` | `mx2.zoho.com` | 20 |
| MX | `@` | `mx3.zoho.com` | 50 |
| TXT | `@` | `v=spf1 include:zoho.com ~all` | n/a |
| TXT | `@` | `zoho-verification=<value Zoho issues>` | n/a |

Tradeoff, and it is the reason this is not the recommendation: Zoho's free plan restricts IMAP/POP/SMTP access, so pulling the mail into Gmail or sending as the alias from Gmail is not reliably available without paying. That defeats the entire point, which was that Root does not want a second inbox to check. Verify the current free-plan terms before choosing this; if free SMTP has returned, Zoho becomes competitive.

**Google Workspace (paid, roughly 7 USD per month).** The only option that fully solves both directions: `hello@oddbatch.app` becomes a real Google mailbox that sends and receives as itself, with the Gmail interface Root already uses. Records:

| Type | Name | Value | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| MX | `@` | `smtp.google.com` | 1 |
| TXT | `@` | `v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all` | n/a |
| TXT | `@` | `google-site-verification=<value Google issues>` | n/a |

Tradeoff: a recurring bill for a landing page that receives maybe four emails a month, plus a second Google account to manage. Not justified at v0. This is the upgrade path once volume is real, not the starting point.

**Fastmail (roughly 5 USD per month).** Same shape as Google Workspace, cheaper, better product, but it is a separate mail client rather than the Gmail Root already lives in. Only preferable if Root wants to leave Gmail, which is not on the table.

**Do not** put a contact form in front of email at v0. More code, more spam surface, worse for the sender, and the whole point of the mailto is that it costs nothing to run.

#### Decision summary

Cloudflare Email Routing if the nameservers can move to Cloudflare. ImprovMX if they cannot. Google Workspace later if replying as `hello@` ever matters. Do not use Zoho unless the free-tier SMTP restriction has been lifted.

## 6. Growth path

### v0, launch (target: one working session)

One page, static, live on the domain. Shelf holds either the empty state or two to three hardcoded entries per section 2. Mailto submission. OG image. Nothing else.

Definition of done: `oddbatch.app` returns 200 over HTTPS with the hero copy present, the OG card renders correctly in a link preview, and `hello@oddbatch.app` receives a test message end to end.

### v1, submissions (only when v0 has been live and getting mail)

Trigger to start: mailto submissions become annoying to process, meaning more than roughly five a week arriving in inconsistent shapes.

Build: a real form on the same page, posting to a Next.js route handler, writing to Supabase.

Supabase specifics, matching Root's existing pattern: use the shared project `lizdpjytdmpbgxchdgrs` with an `ob_` table prefix. One table, `ob_submissions`, with columns for name, url, one-line description, platform, submitter email, status (`pending` / `listed` / `declined`), and created_at. RLS on, insert-only for the anon key, no public read. Reviewing is done in the Supabase dashboard, not in a custom admin UI.

Do not use Formspree or a Google Form. Formspree is a third-party dependency and a monthly bill for something a route handler does in 30 lines, and a Google Form breaks the visual register of the page completely.

TDD applies from here: validation logic (URL shape, length limits, honeypot, rate limit) gets tests written first.

Spam handling: a honeypot field and a per-IP rate limit are enough at this scale. No captcha. A captcha is a wall on a page whose whole point is being low-friction.

### v2, real directory (only when the shelf outgrows one screen)

Trigger to start: more than roughly 25 listed apps, at which point a flat list stops being scannable.

Build: move shelf entries from `content/apps.ts` to an `ob_apps` table, add tags, add client-side filter and search over the full list (no server search at this size), and give each app a page at `/a/[slug]` with the description, link, and maker credit. Add `ItemList` structured data. Add an RSS feed of newly shelved apps, which is the single highest-value retention feature for this audience and costs almost nothing.

### What not to build, ever or nearly

- User accounts, logins, or profiles.
- Voting, upvotes, scores, rankings, "trending". These convert a shelf into a leaderboard and destroy the neutrality that is the entire positioning.
- Comments or reviews.
- A newsletter, until there is a reason for one and someone willing to write it. A dead newsletter signup is worse than none.
- Affiliate or referral links. Section 2's copy explicitly promises no cut. Breaking that later is a credibility fire.
- Paid or sponsored placement.
- A blog.
- Any AI feature. "AI-powered app discovery" is the exact thing this brand is a reaction against.
- Dark mode before there is content.
- A CMS.

The discipline rule for future sessions: if a proposed feature would make oddbatch look like it has a business model, it is out.

## 7. Instrumentation

**Analytics: Vercel Web Analytics, enabled from the dashboard.**

Reasoning: it is one toggle plus one component import, no third-party script domain, no cookie banner obligation since it does not set cookies or store personal identifiers, and it answers the only questions worth asking at this size (does anyone visit, and where from). Plausible is a better product but costs around 9 USD per month for a page that will get 200 visits in its first month, and self-hosting Plausible for a one-pager is absurd. Google Analytics is off the table: it contradicts the brand and it drags a consent banner onto a page whose defining quality is that it is quiet.

Note that adding the Vercel Analytics component means one client component and roughly 1KB of JS, which is a real, small violation of the zero-JS rule in CLAUDE.md. It is worth it. Note the exception explicitly in CLAUDE.md so it does not become precedent.

The honest alternative: **no analytics at all** for v0, reading Vercel's built-in request logs instead when curious. This is defensible and it is the most on-brand choice. Take it if Root would rather not think about numbers. Section 9 asks.

**Uptime monitoring: skip.**

A statically served page on Vercel's edge has no runtime, no database, and no dependency that can fail independently of Vercel itself. Monitoring it monitors Vercel. Revisit at v1, when a route handler and a Supabase connection introduce something that can actually break, and at that point monitor the submission endpoint specifically rather than the homepage.

**Error tracking: skip at v0.** There is no JavaScript to throw. Add Sentry at v1 if the submission route justifies it, which it probably will not.
